# v3.2.0 — Aerobic efficiency

The dashboard now shows whether you are getting fitter, not only whether you are training: how fast
you run at your usual heart rate, how that moved across the 16 weeks on screen, and how it compares
with the same weeks a year earlier.

## Added

- **Aerobic efficiency card**, under Weekly distance. The headline reads your latest pace at the
  window's usual heart rate — for example "6:29 /km at 148 bpm" — and how many seconds per km faster
  or slower that is than at the start of the window. Numbers only: no colours, scores, or advice.
- **The usual heart rate is your own.** It is the median average heart rate of the runs in the window
  on screen, so the card compares like with like within that window and against last year.
- **One dot per run** at its pace restated at that heart rate, and a line that follows the median of
  each week and the three before it. The line breaks where there are too few runs; it never fills a
  gap.
- **Last year's line**: the same weeks a year earlier, dashed, read at this window's heart rate. It is
  absent when no run is stored in those weeks.
- **Fair to hills.** Only runs of at least 3 km with both a pace and a heart rate count. A gently
  hilly run is adjusted for its climb and drawn hollow; a steep run is drawn with its own marker but
  never moves the line. A run far above your usual heart rate is left out.
- **Too few runs, said plainly.** With fewer than 8 qualifying runs in the window, the card says how
  many it found instead of drawing a trend.
- **Accessible chart.** It has a text alternative listing every week's dates, pace, last year's pace,
  and run count; markers and lines differ by shape and dash, not by colour alone.

## Changed

- **Previous and Next move the card with the map**: each window gets its own usual heart rate, line,
  and last year's line.
- **A new import or sync refreshes the card** on every window, like the map and the weekly trend.

## After upgrading

- Nothing to run: there is no database migration and no new setting. The card uses the pace, heart
  rate, and climb your runs already store. Rebuild the configuration cache if your deployment keeps
  one; it does on every regular deploy.
